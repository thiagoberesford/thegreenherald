UN Negotiators Move Closer to Global Plastics Treaty
After years of talks, diplomats inch toward a legally binding agreement to end plastic pollution by 2040.
Delegates at the Intergovernmental Negotiating Committee on Plastic Pollution, convened by UNEP, made progress over the weekend toward a landmark treaty that would mandate reductions in plastic production and waste. The draft text now includes binding targets for 2035 and 2040, though key disputes remain over enforcement and financing. Environmental groups welcomed the momentum but warned that corporate lobbying could dilute the agreement. "This is a once-in-a-generation chance to break free from the plastic crisis," said a spokesperson for the Break Free From Plastic coalition. The final round of negotiations is scheduled for December 2026.

[China] China Accelerates Clean Energy Transition with New Five-Year Plan
Beijing unveils sweeping measures to cut coal dependence and scale up renewables.
China’s State Council released a draft five-year plan that sets a 25% non-fossil fuel share in primary energy consumption by 2025, up from 16% in 2020. The plan includes stricter emissions standards for coal plants and a 50% increase in solar and wind capacity additions. Analysts note the shift reflects both climate commitments and energy security concerns amid volatile global fuel markets.

[Canada] SBTi Approves Cascades’ 2035 Greenhouse Gas Reduction Targets
Pulp and paper giant commits to near-term decarbonization aligned with 1.5°C pathway.
The Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi) has validated Cascades’ plan to cut absolute Scope 1 and 2 emissions by 42% and Scope 3 emissions by 25% by 2035, against a 2021 baseline. The targets cover operations across North America, including recycled paper and packaging facilities. "This approval underscores our commitment to leading the industry toward a low-carbon future," said Cascades’ sustainability director.
