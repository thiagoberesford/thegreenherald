Climate Scientists Turn to AI to Decode Extreme Weather Patterns
New research reveals how artificial intelligence is accelerating climate modeling and disaster prediction.
Climate scientists are increasingly leveraging artificial intelligence to analyze vast datasets and improve predictions of extreme weather events. A new report from Carbon Brief highlights how AI models are being trained to identify patterns in historical climate data, offering more accurate forecasts for hurricanes, heatwaves, and floods. "AI is not replacing climate science, but it’s giving us tools to ask questions we couldn’t before," said one researcher. The integration of AI into climate modeling is seen as a critical step in adapting to a warming planet. By automating data analysis, scientists can focus on interpreting results and developing mitigation strategies. Experts warn, however, that AI’s effectiveness depends on the quality of input data and the transparency of algorithms used.
