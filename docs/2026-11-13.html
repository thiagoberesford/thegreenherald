Global Warming Remains Unchecked, Experts Warn at UN’s COP31 Climate Summit
Nations fail to meet 2030 targets as COP31 opens with calls for urgent, binding action.
The UN’s COP31 climate summit in Sydney opened with stark warnings that global warming is accelerating beyond control, as nations fall short of their 2030 emissions targets. Scientists and diplomats described the gap between pledges and reality as "alarming," with no major economy on track to limit temperature rise to 1.5°C. Delegates from vulnerable nations demanded immediate, legally binding commitments, while fossil fuel lobbyists faced pushback over continued subsidies. "We are not just off track—we are in a ditch," said a Pacific Islands representative. The summit’s first week will focus on securing finance for adaptation and loss-and-damage funds.

Global climate policies continue to advance despite setbacks, research finds
Study reveals steady progress in climate legislation and enforcement worldwide.
A new analysis published in *Phys.org* shows that despite high-profile policy rollbacks and political resistance, global climate policies have continued to advance in recent years. Researchers tracked over 2,000 legislative and regulatory changes across 195 countries, finding that 62% of nations strengthened their climate frameworks since 2020. The study highlights that subnational governments and courts are increasingly filling gaps left by national inaction.
