Q&A: What is the Belém-Antalya mechanism for a ‘just transition’?
The proposed framework aims to bridge divides between developed and developing nations, but its practicality remains under scrutiny.
The Belém-Antalya mechanism, unveiled ahead of COP31, seeks to create a pathway for ‘just transitions’ by linking climate finance with social equity goals. Proponents argue it could address historical imbalances in climate responsibility, while critics question whether it provides enforceable commitments or merely symbolic gestures. The mechanism’s design, still under negotiation, will be a litmus test for its viability.

5 ways countries stymie climate negotiations without actually saying ‘no’
From ‘constructive ambiguity’ to ‘procedural delays,’ nations deploy subtle tactics to derail progress at COP summits.
Negotiators at COP31 are bracing for a familiar playbook of obstruction, where countries use tactics like ‘constructive ambiguity’—leaving agreements vague enough to avoid outright rejection—or ‘procedural delays’ to stall decisions. Other strategies include ‘cherry-picking’ commitments to align only with national interests or ‘forum shopping’ to shift discussions to less scrutinized venues. These maneuvers, while not explicit vetoes, can dilute ambition over time.
