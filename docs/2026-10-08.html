High Court’s landmark climate ruling counts true cost of coal
Landmark decision forces governments to factor in health and environmental damages in fossil fuel assessments.
A landmark High Court ruling has set a precedent by requiring governments to include the true cost of coal—health harms, environmental degradation, and climate impacts—in their economic assessments of fossil fuel projects. The decision follows a case that exposed how traditional cost-benefit analyses systematically undercount the externalities of coal. Legal experts say the ruling could reshape how policymakers evaluate energy projects globally, particularly in countries still expanding coal infrastructure. Environmental groups hailed it as a turning point for climate accountability, while industry representatives warned of potential delays in energy planning.
